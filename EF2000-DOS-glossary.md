# EF2000 (MS-DOS, 1995) — Military, Aviation and Technical Glossary

Companion to [**EF2000 (MS-DOS) — Keyboard Shortcuts**](https://chatgpt.com/library?file_id=libfile_fd80b52fdf6481918877446da5988193&account_id=personal). This glossary explains the vocabulary in that reference and the wider game: aircraft handling, cockpit instruments, radar, defensive systems, weapons, combat tactics, missions, campaign operations and the DOS simulator itself.

**Scope and accuracy:** This is about **Digital Image Design's 1995 *EF2000***, including its later DOS patches and *TACTCOM*, **not** the 2001 *Eurofighter Typhoon* game or a modern Typhoon simulator. EF2000 depicts a *projected* Eurofighter and a fictional Scandinavian war. Its original manual expressly notes that its systems are interpretations based on then-public information, not necessarily those of the final aircraft. Definitions marked **[general]** explain relevant aviation/military vocabulary that is useful for understanding EF2000 but is **not a claim that the game explicitly simulates that feature**. Other entries may also simplify complicated real-world systems for game use.

The sections follow cockpit functions and mission phases, with entries **roughly alphabetized within each section**. Abbreviations are expanded at first mention, including terms familiar to experienced flight-sim pilots.

## 1. Aircraft, flight controls and aerodynamics

- **Afterburner / reheat (AB)** — Extra fuel burned in a jet engine's exhaust stream for substantially more thrust and fuel consumption. EF2000 offers separate afterburner controls.
- **Airbrake / speed brake** — Movable surface that adds drag to slow the aircraft without relying entirely on engine power; different from the landing wheel brakes.
- **Aileron** — Wing control surface primarily used to roll an aircraft. On modern fly-by-wire fighters the computer combines several surfaces to produce a requested motion.
- **Angle of attack (AoA / AOA)** — Angle between the wing's reference line and the oncoming airflow, *not* the aircraft's angle above the horizon. High AoA enables strong turning but can increase drag or produce a stall.
- **Attitude** — Aircraft orientation: pitch, bank/roll and heading relative to the horizon.
- **Bank** — Tilt of the aircraft's wings from horizontal. Banking changes the lift direction and normally initiates a turn.
- **Canard** — Small foreplane ahead of the main wing. EF2000's canard-delta arrangement contributes to pitch control and maneuverability.
- **Control authority** — How effectively control surfaces or flight-control software can change aircraft attitude.
- **Delta wing** — Broad triangular wing; part of the Eurofighter's design.
- **Drag** — Aerodynamic force opposing movement through air. Speed, external stores, airbrakes and flight attitude affect drag.
- **Drag chute / brake parachute** — Parachute deployed after landing to shorten the ground roll; simulated through the game's brake-chute commands.
- **Ejection seat / eject** — Emergency system that propels the pilot clear of a doomed aircraft; in EF2000 this is a game command, separate from simply quitting a mission.
- **Elevator / elevon** — A surface commanding pitch; an *elevon* combines pitch and roll functions, especially on delta wings.
- **Energy / energy state** — An aircraft's combination of speed and altitude: kinetic energy plus potential energy. A crucial concept in dogfighting.
- **External stores** — Weapons, fuel tanks or equipment carried outside the airframe, usually increasing weight and drag.
- **Flight envelope** — Safe or modeled combinations of speed, altitude, AoA and acceleration in which the aircraft can fly.
- **Fly-by-wire (FBW)** — Flight-control computer interprets pilot input and commands control surfaces electronically. In the game it helps model the Eurofighter's otherwise demanding aerodynamics.
- **G / g-force / load factor** — Acceleration felt by the aircraft and pilot, expressed in multiples of normal gravity. A high-G turn can cause pilot blackout or stress the aircraft.
- **High-alpha** — Flying at a high angle of attack; useful for pointing the nose, but may sacrifice speed.
- **Lift** — Aerodynamic force generated by wings, broadly perpendicular to relative airflow; required to support the aircraft and turn.
- **Mach number** — Speed relative to the local speed of sound: Mach 1 is sonic speed, Mach 2 twice sonic speed. The speed of sound changes with atmospheric conditions.
- **Pitch** — Nose-up/nose-down rotation around the aircraft's lateral axis.
- **Roll** — Rotation about the nose-to-tail axis, tilting the wings.
- **Rudder** — Control surface on the vertical tail used to command yaw. EF2000 also maps rudder keys to nose-wheel steering when on the ground.
- **Stall** — Loss of the wing's normal lifting behavior after critical AoA is exceeded; not simply an engine stopping. A stall can happen at many speeds.
- **Thrust** — Forward force from engines; afterburning greatly increases it.
- **Throttle** — Engine power control. Reducing throttle does not necessarily reduce speed immediately, especially during a dive.
- **Toe brakes** — Independent wheel-brake controls operated by each foot in a real aircraft, enabling directional braking while taxiing. EF2000 offers separate left/right brake keys.
- **Thrust-to-weight ratio** — Engine thrust divided by aircraft weight; affects acceleration and climb performance.
- **Trim [general]** — Small persistent control adjustment used to relieve steady control pressure; often largely automated on FBW aircraft.
- **Velocity vector / flight-path marker** — Symbol showing where the aircraft is *actually moving*, which may differ from where its nose points.
- **Yaw** — Left/right rotation of the nose around the aircraft's vertical axis.

## 2. Takeoff, landing, navigation and aerial refuelling

- **AGL (Above Ground Level)** — Height above local terrain. Especially important for low-level attack and terrain clearance; distinct from height above sea level.
- **Approach** — Final sequence of maneuvers setting up a landing at a runway or a rendezvous with a tanker.
- **Barometric altitude** — Altitude estimated from air pressure, typically relative to a sea-level reference. In EF2000 it can differ dramatically from height above nearby terrain.
- **Autolevel / auto-leveller** — EF2000 flight-assist feature that attempts to recover a level attitude; separate from the four-mode autopilot.
- **Autopilot (AP)** — Automatic flight-control function. EF2000 has **waypoint follow**, **heading/altitude/speed**, **target tracking**, and **autothrottle** modes.
- **Autothrottle** — Automatic adjustment of engine thrust toward a selected airspeed; in EF2000 it is the fourth AP mode.
- **Bearing** — Direction from one point to another, usually measured clockwise in degrees from north.
- **Bingo fuel [general]** — Predetermined fuel quantity at which a pilot must return or divert, allowing a reserve.
- **Brake / wheel brake** — Ground braking using the landing wheels; unlike the airbrake, which works aerodynamically.
- **Closure / closure rate** — Relative speed of two aircraft along their line of approach. A high rate makes formation, combat and tanker rendezvous difficult.
- **Compass** — Indicator of direction and heading.
- **Course** — Intended path across the ground; differs from the aircraft's nose direction when wind causes drift.
- **Drogue / basket** — Flexible funnel on a tanker hose that the receiving aircraft connects to with a probe.
- **Final / final approach** — Last, normally straight portion of a landing approach aligned with the runway.
- **Flight level [general]** — Standard-pressure altitude expressed in hundreds of feet (e.g., FL300 = nominal 30,000 ft).
- **Heading** — Direction the aircraft's nose points, usually in degrees relative to north; not necessarily its ground track.
- **ILS (Instrument Landing System)** — Radio-based guidance for landing that provides alignment and descent cues. `I` changes EF2000 to its landing/ILS HUD mode; do not assume the simulation models every real ILS detail.
- **Initial point (IP)** — Preplanned point where an attack begins or a flight changes from transit to tactical operation.
- **KIAS / indicated airspeed** — Knots indicated by an aircraft's airspeed instrument. It is not always the same as true airspeed or ground speed.
- **Landing gear / undercarriage** — Wheels and supporting mechanism used for takeoff and landing; retracted in flight.
- **MSL / AMSL (Mean Sea Level / Above Mean Sea Level)** — Altitude relative to sea level. At a given MSL altitude terrain may be dangerously close.
- **Navigation leg** — Route segment between consecutive waypoints.
- **Nose-wheel steering** — Steering the aircraft's front wheel during taxiing and the ground roll.
- **Probe-and-drogue refuelling** — Air-to-air refuelling system where a probe on the receiving jet meets a drogue on a trailing hose. EF2000 has probe and refuelling-HUD controls.
- **Radar altimeter (RADALT)** — Measures height directly above terrain by radio ranging. EF2000's displayed altitude can switch between radar height and barometric altitude, so a sudden large numerical change near mountains is possible.
- **Rendezvous** — Meeting another aircraft at a specified location/time, notably the refuelling tanker.
- **RTB (Return to Base)** — Leave the mission area and fly back to an airfield.
- **Runway heading** — Magnetic or true direction aligned with a runway; displayed in EF2000's landing guidance.
- **Taxi / taxiing** — Moving on the ground under aircraft power, before takeoff or after landing.
- **Tanker** — Aircraft carrying fuel for mid-air transfer; EF2000 includes refuelling encounters.
- **Time on Target (TOT)** — Planned time the attacking aircraft or weapon reaches a target; used in mission timing.
- **Track / ground track** — Direction of movement over the Earth's surface; may differ from heading because of wind.
- **Waypoint (WP)** — Named or numbered route point. EF2000's navigation and waypoint-following AP use these; some mission points mark refuelling, initiation and landing.

## 3. Cockpit, displays and visual aids

- **Artificial horizon** — Instrument showing aircraft pitch and bank relative to the horizon; available as a separate EF2000 panel.
- **Avionics** — Aircraft's electronic systems: radar, sensors, navigation, displays, communications and defensive equipment.
- **Boresight** — Direction straight ahead along the aircraft's nose or sensor axis; useful as a reference for radar, HUD and weapon aiming.
- **Declutter** — Suppress less essential display symbols to make critical information easier to read. EF2000 has a HUD declutter command.
- **FOV (Field of View)** — Angular width of the scene shown by a cockpit camera or display. Wide-angle views trade apparent size for peripheral awareness.
- **Head-down display (HDD)** — Instrument screen viewed by looking down into the cockpit; EF2000's three MFDs are examples.
- **HMD (Helmet-Mounted Display)** — Aiming/information cues associated with where the pilot looks. EF2000's close-combat/padlock view represents helmet-directed high-off-boresight ASRAAM targeting; this is not the same display as the forward HUD.
- **HUD (Head-Up Display)** — Flight/weapon data overlaid in the pilot's forward view: heading, speed, altitude, aiming and steering cues.
- **HUD repeater / overlay** — Duplicate of selected sensor, radar, DASS or tactical-map information superimposed on the forward view; available differently across DOS versions.
- **IR/TV image** — Infrared or television-like targeting imagery displayed by systems such as TIALD; images and symbols may represent modeled rather than photographic sensor output.
- **MFD (Multi-Function Display)** — Screen that can show several modes rather than one fixed instrument. EF2000 uses left, centre and right MFDs.
- **MFD 1 / left MFD** — Primarily the radar and AGM-65 Maverick seeker display in EF2000.
- **MFD 2 / centre/map MFD** — Navigation map, tactical JTIDS information and TIALD targeting view in EF2000.
- **MFD 3 / right MFD** — Mission, autopilot, DASS, fuel, engine and system-status functions in EF2000.
- **MMD (Moving Map Display)** — Term for the EF2000 moving-map/tactical-navigation display, particularly the centre MFD.
- **Padlock view** — Virtual camera that keeps looking at a selected aircraft or missile even while the player maneuvers; a viewing aid, **not** a radar lock.
- **Pipper / reticle** — Aiming mark for a gun, bomb or missile. In some EF2000 gun modes a valid pipper/target alignment triggers autofire.
- **Pitch ladder** — HUD lines at known pitch angles, helping assess attitude relative to the horizon.
- **SA (Situational Awareness)** — Understanding own position and condition, the locations/intentions of threats and friendlies, and what may happen next.
- **Softkey** — Physical or simulated button whose action depends on the current display screen/menu. EF2000's `Ctrl+letter` shortcuts activate contextual MFD softkeys.
- **Symbology** — Standardized marks, numbers, brackets and shapes used on electronic displays to communicate information.
- **VR / virtual cockpit** — Game view that permits looking around a modeled 3D cockpit. In this 1995 game, *VR* does not mean a modern head-mounted VR headset.
- **Warning panel** — Cockpit display collecting caution/warning messages about aircraft systems.
- **Zoom** — Magnification of a camera or sensor display, helping examine a target at the expense of context.

## 4. Radar, target tracking and data links

- **Air picture** — Overall representation of nearby air traffic and threats, built from sensors and shared tactical data.
- **Azimuth** — Horizontal angle left/right from a reference direction. Radar azimuth controls vary the surveyed sector.
- **BVR (Beyond Visual Range)** — Air combat conducted at distances where pilots cannot reliably see the enemy with the naked eye; depends on radar/data links and missiles.
- **Closing speed** — Rate at which range to a target decreases; can be positive or negative and is **not** simply the target's airspeed.
- **Contact / plot** — Sensor indication that something is present; it may not yet be positively identified or reliably tracked.
- **Doppler radar** — Radar using frequency shifts in reflected signals to infer radial motion and separate moving targets from background returns.
- **ECR-90** — Name used for the Eurofighter's planned multimode radar as depicted in 1995 EF2000. The game provides automatic tracking, scan/sector selection and target prioritization; do not equate its behavior with later real-world radar variants.
- **Elevation / radar scan elevation** — Vertical direction in which the radar searches: high, level or low relative to the aircraft.
- **FLIR (Forward-Looking Infrared)** — Infrared imaging viewed ahead of the aircraft; related in purpose to thermal/IRST sensors, but the exact capabilities differ. **[general]** Do not assume EF2000 models the later Typhoon's full FLIR suite.
- **Ground clutter** — Radar returns from land or sea that may obscure airborne contacts, particularly when looking downward.
- **IFF (Identification Friend or Foe)** — Electronic cooperative-identification system used to help distinguish friendly units; a sensor contact alone does not guarantee identity.
- **Intercept** — Maneuver to reach an aircraft's predicted position, usually to identify or engage it.
- **IRST (Infrared Search and Track)** — Passive sensor that detects and tracks aircraft through their infrared emissions; unlike active radar it need not emit radio waves.
- **JSTARS / J-STARS (Joint Surveillance Target Attack Radar System)** — Airborne radar surveillance system primarily for tracking ground activity; an external information source represented in EF2000's tactical picture.
- **JTIDS (Joint Tactical Information Distribution System)** — Military tactical data link used to share tracks and other situation data. EF2000's JTIDS display shows reported contacts beyond those directly seen by the ownship radar.
- **Lock-on / target lock** — Sensor or weapon tracking a selected target well enough to support an engagement. The needed lock type differs by weapon.
- **Look-down / shoot-down** — Detecting and attacking an aircraft below the radar-carrying fighter, against a cluttered ground background.
- **Mile-range / range scale** — Maximum distance shown on a radar or map display. Scale changes affect how much area is visible, not the true weapon range.
- **Moving map** — Navigation display that depicts terrain and geographic features with the aircraft's route and position.
- **Ownship** — The player's aircraft, as distinct from other aircraft displayed as tracks.
- **Passive sensor** — Detects energy emitted by others without deliberately transmitting the primary detection signal. IRST and RWR are examples.
- **Pulse-Doppler radar** — Active radar combining pulsed ranging with Doppler processing for target detection, especially against clutter.
- **Radar (Radio Detection and Ranging)** — System that emits radio waves and analyzes returned echoes to estimate location, distance and sometimes velocity.
- **Radar horizon** — Range beyond which curvature of the Earth prevents direct line-of-sight radar detection, especially at low altitude.
- **RCS (Radar Cross-Section)** — Measure of how strongly a target reflects radar energy, not simply its geometric size.
- **Scan / sweep** — Search motion or electronic steering of a sensor through a specified volume.
- **Sector** — Angular area selected for radar scanning, typically in azimuth and elevation.
- **Sensor fusion [general]** — Combining multiple sensor and data-link reports into a more coherent tactical picture.
- **TWS (Track While Scan)** — Radar mode/ability to maintain tracks on targets while continuing to search the surrounding area. EF2000's ECR-90 automates much of this process.
- **Target allocation / prioritization** — Assigning relative importance to contacts and choosing which aircraft/weapon should engage which target.
- **Track** — Sensor-estimated target position and movement updated over time; distinct from ownship's navigation track.
- **WVR (Within Visual Range)** — Close-in combat where opposing aircraft may be seen directly; missiles and guns remain important.

## 5. Threat detection, countermeasures and electronic warfare

- **AAA (Anti-Aircraft Artillery)** — Guns intended to fire at aircraft from the ground or ships; includes rapid-fire automatic weapons.
- **Active / passive defense** — *Active* measures interfere with or defeat an attack (jamming, countermeasures, maneuvering); *passive* measures reduce exposure (terrain masking, routing, emission control).
- **ARM (Anti-Radiation Missile)** — Weapon that homes on radar or other radio-frequency emitters. The game's ALARM is a specific example.
- **Burn-through [general]** — Range at which an active radar return becomes strong enough to detect a target despite jamming.
- **Chaff** — Clouds of small radar-reflective strips or fibers deployed to confuse radar seekers or tracking systems. EF2000 supports manual releases.
- **Countermeasure** — Device or tactic used to reduce the effectiveness of enemy sensing or weapons, including chaff, flares and electronic jamming.
- **DASS (Defensive Aids Sub-System)** — EF2000's integrated threat-awareness and defensive suite: warnings, radar-threat indications, countermeasures and electronic warfare controls. Selecting its MFD page and toggling the system are distinct concepts.
- **Decoy** — False or more attractive target introduced to mislead a hostile sensor or missile; chaff and flares are expendable decoys.
- **ECM (Electronic Countermeasures)** — Electronic measures that degrade enemy sensors, especially by transmitting radio-frequency jamming signals. EF2000 has an ECM on/off command.
- **ECCM (Electronic Counter-Countermeasures)** — Sensor/weapon techniques designed to keep working despite enemy ECM; general military term.
- **Electronic warfare (EW)** — Use of the electromagnetic spectrum to detect, disrupt, deceive or protect against an opponent's equipment.
- **Emitter** — Radar, jammer or communication equipment transmitting detectable electromagnetic energy.
- **Emission control (EMCON) [general]** — Limiting radar and radio transmissions to reduce the chance of detection; passive IRST and shared JTIDS tracks can help maintain awareness.
- **Flare** — Bright infrared decoy used against heat-seeking missiles. Distinct from chaff, which primarily affects radar.
- **GPWS (Ground Proximity Warning System) [general]** — Equipment that warns of dangerously close terrain or obstacles. Relevant to modern fighter avionics, but do not assume EF2000 has a complete real-world GPWS simulation.
- **Heat seeker / IR-guided missile** — Missile following infrared radiation from a target; vulnerable to flares rather than radar chaff alone.
- **Jamming** — Interfering with an enemy receiver by transmitting disruptive or deceptive signals; using it can also reveal one's presence.
- **Laser warning [general]** — Indication that an aircraft is being illuminated by a laser designator or rangefinder.
- **Line of sight (LOS)** — Unobstructed viewing path from sensor to target. Hills and Earth curvature can block radar, visual and laser sensing.
- **Missile-approach warning** — System alerting a pilot to an incoming missile, allowing countermeasures and an evasive maneuver.
- **Radar homing** — Missile guidance based on radar energy: *semi-active* uses illumination from another radar, while *active* carries its own radar seeker.
- **RWR (Radar Warning Receiver)** — Passive receiver that detects and classifies radar emissions from aircraft, surface defenses and ships; central to DASS threat awareness.
- **SAM (Surface-to-Air Missile)** — Missile launched from the ground or a ship against aircraft, usually part of an air-defense system.
- **SAM site / battery** — Group of launchers, search/tracking radar and supporting equipment capable of engaging aircraft.
- **Search radar / early-warning radar (EWR)** — Radar scanning a wide area for possible threats, generally before a specialized tracking/fire-control system engages them.
- **SEAD (Suppression of Enemy Air Defenses)** — Mission to disable, destroy or temporarily disrupt enemy SAMs, radars and associated command systems; ALARM is a relevant EF2000 weapon.
- **Terrain masking / nap-of-the-earth (NOE) [general]** — Flying behind terrain to hide from radar and weapons; it reduces visibility but raises collision risk.
- **Threat ring / threat envelope** — Approximate area in which an enemy defense system can detect or engage an aircraft; boundaries are not guarantees of safety.
- **Tracking radar / fire-control radar** — Radar maintaining precise target information so a gun or missile can attack it; typically more threatening than a broad-area search radar.
- **Warning tone / lock warning** — Audio or visual indication of a threat or sensor condition, requiring the pilot to interpret the source and urgency.

## 6. Weapons, stores and air-to-air combat equipment

- **AA / A/A / A2A (Air-to-Air)** — Aircraft attacking aircraft; in EF2000 it is also a weapon-selection and HUD mode.
- **AIM (Air Intercept Missile)** — U.S. designation prefix for air-to-air missiles (e.g., AIM-9 and AIM-120); the number identifies a family, not the launch mode.
- **AIM-9M Sidewinder** — Short-range infrared-guided air-to-air missile in EF2000. It uses a heat-seeking lock, commonly indicated by an acquisition tone.
- **AIM-120 AMRAAM (Advanced Medium-Range Air-to-Air Missile)** — Radar-guided beyond-visual-range missile in EF2000; real AMRAAM uses an active radar seeker in its terminal phase.
- **AIM-132 ASRAAM (Advanced Short-Range Air-to-Air Missile)** — Agile infrared-guided missile for close-range engagements, represented in EF2000.
- **All-aspect** — Ability of a seeker to acquire a target from more than just the rear; does not mean every firing angle has equally good hit probability.
- **AMRAAM** — See **AIM-120 AMRAAM**. A weapon family, not a general name for all radar-guided missiles.
- **Aspect / aspect angle** — Orientation of a target relative to the attacking aircraft, particularly whether it is showing its nose, flank or tail. Crucial to missile effectiveness.
- **Boresight launch [general]** — Weapon launch using a seeker or aiming line aligned near the aircraft's nose, rather than a separately cued off-axis target.
- **BK-27 / Mauser BK-27** — Eurofighter's 27 mm aircraft cannon, represented as an internal gun in EF2000; *BK* denotes *Bordkanone* (onboard cannon).
- **Cannon / gun** — Internal rapid-fire weapon for close-range air or ground targets. EF2000 has a cannon selection and gun-aiming mode.
- **Hardpoint / station** — Structural mounting position for an external weapon, tank or pod. Not every store fits every station.
- **Infrared (IR) seeker** — Sensor in a missile that follows heat signatures; used by Sidewinder and ASRAAM.
- **Jettison** — Deliberately discard a tank or store, typically to improve performance or deal with an emergency; does not imply detonating the weapon.
- **Launch envelope** — Conditions under which a weapon has a reasonable chance of working: range, speed, altitude and target geometry.
- **LRAAM / S-225** — Long-range air-to-air missile label used for the S-225 carried in EF2000's projected arsenal. Do not confuse it with a currently fielded Typhoon weapon.
- **Missile seeker** — Sensor system that identifies and tracks the intended target after or before launch, using IR, radar or another signal.
- **P_k / PK (Probability of Kill)** — Estimated chance that one weapon shot will destroy a target under given conditions; depends on range, aspect, speeds and defenses.
- **Pylon** — Support structure connecting an external store to an aircraft's hardpoint.
- **Radar-guided missile** — Guided by radar information, which may be supplied by the launching platform, the missile's own seeker or both.
- **Rear-aspect shot** — Firing toward the back of a target aircraft; often favorable for early IR-guided missiles.
- **Semi-active radar homing (SARH)** — Guidance in which a missile follows radar energy reflected from a target illuminated by another radar. Distinct from active radar homing.
- **Shoot cue / launch authorization** — HUD indication that the current lock and geometry meet a simulated weapon-release condition; *lock alone* does not ensure a hit.
- **Stores management** — Selection, monitoring, firing and jettisoning of weapons or fuel tanks.
- **Weapon-eye / missile camera** — Game view following a released weapon for observation; this is a camera feature, not evidence that every modeled weapon transmits real video.

## 7. Air-to-ground, anti-radar and anti-ship weapons

- **AG / A/G / A2G (Air-to-Ground)** — Aircraft attacking surface targets, including vehicles, buildings, radar sites and ships; also EF2000's ground-weapon/HUD selection.
- **AGM (Air-to-Ground Missile)** — U.S. designation prefix for missiles intended to strike surface targets; AGM-65 is the EF2000 example.
- **AGM-65D Maverick** — Thermal-imaging guided air-to-ground missile against surface targets. EF2000 presents a target-seeker image on the left MFD; it is **not** a laser-guided bomb.
- **ALARM (Air-Launched Anti-Radiation Missile)** — British missile designed to attack active radar emitters. In EF2000 it has **direct** (with a designated/locked emitter) and **indirect** (searching for emissions after launch) modes.
- **Anti-ship missile (ASM)** — Missile intended to attack vessels; EF2000 includes Sea Eagle.
- **BL755** — British cluster bomb that disperses multiple submunitions over an area, rather than hitting one point with a single large warhead.
- **Bomb release / pickle** — Command to drop a selected bomb; in EF2000 the spacebar can fire or release depending on selected weapon.
- **CCIP (Continuously Computed Impact Point)** — Projected ground impact point based on present flight conditions, shown as a HUD aim/release cue for suitable unguided weapons.
- **Cluster bomb** — Munition that releases many smaller bomblets over a footprint; BL755 is the game example.
- **CRV7** — Canadian-designed aircraft rocket, used in salvos against ground targets in EF2000; unlike a guided missile it does not home on a selected target.
- **Direct/indirect ALARM mode** — Game-specific distinction between engaging a locked emitter immediately and launching an anti-radiation missile to search for emissions over an area.
- **Durandal** — Specialized runway-cratering bomb designed to penetrate and damage pavement; included in EF2000's runway-attack arsenal.
- **GBU (Guided Bomb Unit)** — U.S. designation for guided bombs; guidance may involve laser, GPS or other means, depending on the specific model.
- **GBU-12 / GBU-16 Paveway** — Laser-guided bombs represented in EF2000; guided toward reflected laser energy from a designated surface target. They are unpowered bombs, not missiles.
- **Iron bomb / dumb bomb** — Unguided free-fall bomb that relies on accurate release timing and aircraft geometry.
- **Lasing / laser designation** — Illuminating a target with coded laser light so a compatible guided weapon can steer toward the reflection.
- **LGB (Laser-Guided Bomb)** — Bomb steered toward a laser-designated aimpoint; EF2000 employs TIALD to designate these targets.
- **Mk 82 / Mk 83** — General-purpose unguided bombs in nominal 500-pound and 1,000-pound classes, respectively; both appear in EF2000.
- **Offset aimpoint** — Aiming slightly away from a default target center, e.g. at a particular building or feature, by moving a sensor crosshair.
- **Ordnance** — Weapons and ammunition carried or expended. *Ordinance* is a law or regulation, not the correct spelling for weapons.
- **Paveway** — Family of laser-guidance kits/bombs; EF2000 represents GBU-12 and GBU-16 variants.
- **Run-in / attack run** — Final planned approach to a target while aligning sensors and weapon release parameters.
- **Sea Eagle** — British radar-guided anti-ship missile represented in EF2000; uses the game's specialized surface-targeting radar mode.
- **Slick bomb** — Low-drag free-fall bomb without special retarding fins or parachute; deployed at suitable altitude/distance for escape.
- **Snakeye / retarded bomb [general]** — Bomb with drag-producing fins or other retardation to permit safer low-altitude release; referenced in contemporary EF2000 bombing discussion.
- **Submunition / bomblet** — One of many small explosive items dispersed from a cluster bomb.
- **TIALD (Thermal Imaging Airborne Laser Designator)** — Imaging and laser-designation system, modeled as EF2000's dedicated ground-targeting display. The pilot can slew the IR/TV view and designate targets for LGBs.
- **Warhead** — Explosive or other destructive payload carried by a missile, bomb or projectile.

## 8. Air combat, formations and maneuvers

- **Angle-off / angle-off-tail (AOT)** — Angular difference between a target's flight path and the attacker's line of sight, often used to describe gun or missile geometry.
- **Bandit** — Positively identified enemy aircraft in fighter radio terminology; different from a *bogey*, which is unidentified.
- **Barrel roll** — Helical maneuver combining roll with a changing flight path; a defensive or repositioning maneuver when properly timed.
- **Beam / beaming** — Flying approximately perpendicular to a radar or missile's line of sight; can complicate Doppler tracking or change closure.
- **Bogey** — Unidentified aircraft/contact, not necessarily hostile.
- **Bracket** — Coordinated two-aircraft tactic approaching a threat from separated directions to limit its defensive options.
- **Break / break turn** — Immediate hard defensive turn to evade an attack or incoming missile; typically consumes substantial energy.
- **Check six** — Check directly behind the aircraft, the six-o'clock direction. EF2000 also uses it as a cockpit camera command.
- **Clock position** — Relative bearing convention: 12 o'clock ahead, 3 right, 6 behind, 9 left.
- **Combat spread** — Formation with aircraft separated laterally to improve mutual visibility and support.
- **Defensive split** — Two-ship maneuver in which aircraft separate to force an attacker to choose a target.
- **Disengage** — Break off combat and move away from danger, often to preserve fuel or return to the mission.
- **Dogfight** — Close-range maneuvering air combat, generally within visual range.
- **Double attack** — Coordinated attack tactic where two fighters alternately engage an opponent, preserving mutual support.
- **Drag** — In fighter tactics, deliberately lead an enemy toward a friendly aircraft or away from an important area. **Not** the aerodynamic force of the same name.
- **Element / two-ship** — Pair of aircraft operating together; a common subunit of a larger flight.
- **Engagement** — Period in which opposing forces attempt to detect, maneuver against and attack one another.
- **Fighter sweep** — Mission to search an area for enemy aircraft and engage them to establish air control.
- **Flight** — Group of aircraft tasked together, often with a lead and wingmen; size varies by doctrine and game mission.
- **Formation** — Planned relative arrangement of multiple aircraft, such as line abreast or trail.
- **Fox One / Fox Two / Fox Three [general]** — NATO-style radio calls for a semi-active radar, infrared and active radar missile launch, respectively. Useful jargon, but not a promise that EF2000 reproduces these spoken calls.
- **Guns / guns kill** — Cannon engagement and a simulated cannon hit/kill; guns have much shorter effective ranges than missiles.
- **Head-on / frontal aspect** — Two aircraft approaching nose-to-nose, often creating a high combined closure rate.
- **High yo-yo / low yo-yo** — Vertical maneuvering techniques that trade altitude and speed to adjust turn radius, closure and position behind a target.
- **Immelmann turn** — Half loop followed by a half roll, reversing direction and generally exchanging speed for altitude.
- **Intercept geometry** — Choice of heading and position to meet a moving target efficiently or at an advantageous angle.
- **Jink** — Repeated unpredictable small maneuvers to spoil an opponent's aim, especially against guns.
- **Lead / lag / pure pursuit** — Flight paths relative to a turning target: aiming ahead of it, behind it, or directly at it. They affect closure, gun lead and turning room.
- **Lead aircraft / flight leader** — Aircraft/pilot directing the formation and often issuing wingman orders.
- **Merge** — The moment opposing aircraft pass close together and usually transition into a visual maneuvering fight.
- **Missile evasion** — Combination of maneuvering, countermeasures, terrain use and timing intended to defeat an incoming missile.
- **Offensive / defensive / neutral** — Relative tactical positions: one side has a firing advantage, is being threatened, or neither clearly dominates.
- **Padlock** — See *Padlock view* in section 3. Keeping a camera on an enemy does not by itself select or lock a missile target.
- **Reversal** — Sharp change of turning direction to force an overshoot or regain an advantage.
- **Rollaway** — Defensive turn/roll away from an attacking aircraft or its line of fire; one maneuver discussed in EF2000 flight-combat material.
- **Scissors / flat scissors** — Repeated opposing reversals, usually at relatively low speed, intended to make an overshooting attacker lose position.
- **Sandwich** — Coordinated two-fighter tactic in which opponents maneuver to trap a target between their attack directions.
- **Separation** — Distance between friendly aircraft in formation, or between opponents in combat.
- **Six / six o'clock** — Directly behind an aircraft, a vulnerable position in a close fight.
- **Situational awareness (SA)** — See section 3; requires balancing cockpit instruments and outside observation.
- **Split-S** — Half roll to inverted followed by a descending half loop, reversing direction by exchanging altitude for speed.
- **Tail chase** — Pursuit of a target from behind, typically with low closure and favorable rear-aspect geometry.
- **Turning circle / turn rate** — Size and speed of a sustained turn; tight turns can bleed energy quickly.
- **Vertical scissors** — Series of climbing and diving reversals used in a slow-speed positional fight.
- **Wingman** — Other aircraft/pilot assigned to support the leader; EF2000 includes wingman radio orders and dedicated wingman camera views.

## 9. Military roles, missions, communication and the campaign

- **Air defense** — Fighter aircraft, radars, SAMs and AAA organized to stop hostile aircraft or missiles.
- **Air superiority / air supremacy** — Dominance in the air sufficient to allow friendly operations; *supremacy* denotes a greater degree of freedom from effective opposition.
- **Air wing / squadron** — Organizational groups of military aircraft; an air wing normally contains multiple squadrons, though exact structures differ.
- **AWACS (Airborne Warning and Control System)** — Large radar and command aircraft that detects air traffic over a wide region and can direct friendly fighters. EF2000 shares AWACS-derived tracks through its simulated tactical network.
- **BARCAP (Barrier Combat Air Patrol)** — Combat air patrol positioned to prevent hostile aircraft crossing a defended boundary or reaching a protected asset.
- **BDA (Battle Damage Assessment)** — Determining whether a target was damaged or destroyed after an attack; informs whether reattack is needed.
- **Briefing / debriefing** — Preflight mission plan, targets and threats / postflight record of what happened and mission success.
- **CAP (Combat Air Patrol)** — Fighters assigned to patrol a region and defend it against enemy aircraft.
- **CAS (Close Air Support)** — Air attacks on hostile forces close to friendly ground troops, requiring coordination to avoid friendly fire.
- **Chain of command** — Hierarchy through which orders and information are passed among military units.
- **Callsign** — Spoken identifier for an aircraft, flight or controller during communications; not necessarily the pilot's name.
- **Campaign / dynamic campaign** — Connected missions in a simulated war whose results influence later operations. EF2000 uses WARGEN and SMARTPILOTS for the evolving environment.
- **Combat air patrol (CAP)** — See CAP above. Distinguish from **escort**, where fighters remain with a particular friendly flight.
- **Combat search and rescue (CSAR) [general]** — Operations to recover isolated or downed personnel in hostile territory; not asserted here as a selectable EF2000 mission type.
- **Defection** — Military aircraft or pilot switching allegiance; contemporary EF2000 guides mention defectors among possible air contacts.
- **Diversion / divert [general]** — Sending an aircraft to a different airfield or mission target, often due to fuel, weather or damage.
- **Escort** — Fighters assigned to protect strike aircraft, tankers, transport aircraft or other vulnerable friendly assets.
- **Ferry mission / redeployment** — Moving aircraft to another base rather than attacking a target; one EF2000 escort variant concerns such movement.
- **Front line / FEBA [general]** — Approximate boundary between opposing ground forces; *FEBA* means Forward Edge of the Battle Area.
- **Ground attack** — Broad category of strikes against surface vehicles, buildings, radar and other targets.
- **Interdiction** — Attack on the enemy's movement, logistics or reinforcements before they directly engage friendly troops.
- **Mission objective** — Target or desired outcome specified in the briefing; often distinct from optional kills on the way.
- **Multirole fighter** — Combat aircraft able to perform both air-to-air and air-to-ground missions. Eurofighter is represented as multirole in EF2000.
- **NATO (North Atlantic Treaty Organization)** — Military alliance depicted on the friendly side of EF2000's fictional campaign; also used loosely in weapons and aircraft classifications.
- **Offensive air support (OAS)** — Air operations attacking enemy surface forces to support broader ground/military objectives.
- **Package / strike package** — Group of different flights assigned complementary tasks, such as strike, escort, SEAD and tanker support.
- **Patrol** — Flight along a route or within a defined area to monitor and respond to threats.
- **Recon / reconnaissance** — Gathering information about enemy forces, terrain or targets, rather than necessarily attacking them.
- **ROE (Rules of Engagement) [general]** — Conditions governing when a military unit may identify or use force against a target.
- **SAM suppression / Wild Weasel** — Offensive action against enemy radar-guided air defenses; *Wild Weasel* is a commonly used nickname for the specialized mission.
- **Scramble** — Order for fighters to take off urgently to intercept a detected threat.
- **SMARTPILOTS / SmartPilots** — EF2000's computer-controlled aircraft/pilot behavior system, distinct from WARGEN's larger campaign decisions. It is a branded AI feature, **not** a standard military abbreviation.
- **Sortie** — One military aircraft's individual flight on a mission; a four-aircraft mission involves four aircraft sorties.
- **Strike** — Planned attack against a ground or maritime objective; may be part of a larger package.
- **Tactical / strategic** — *Tactical* concerns local engagements and immediate mission objectives; *strategic* concerns wider war goals and sustained capabilities.
- **TACTCOM** — EF2000's DOS expansion/update associated with further tactical/campaign and interface features. It is a product title, not an aircraft system; this glossary covers terminology across the DOS family.
- **TOW / theatre of war** — Geographic region where an armed conflict is taking place; contemporary EF2000 reporting calls its Scandinavian/Norwegian operational area the *TOW*. Do not confuse with the TOW anti-tank missile acronym.
- **WARGEN (War Generator)** — Digital Image Design's dynamic campaign/war simulation that decides how friendly and opposing forces conduct operations over time.
- **Warsaw Pact** — Former Soviet-led military alliance (dissolved in 1991). EF2000 uses inherited Soviet/Russian equipment and adversary concepts in its alternative-history conflict, **not** a literal 1995 Warsaw Pact.
- **Wingman command menu** — Context-sensitive radio-order interface used to tell AI partners what to do; numbered choices change by submenu.

## 10. Aircraft, forces and target categories

- **AFV (Armoured Fighting Vehicle)** — Military ground vehicle with armor, such as a tank or armored personnel carrier; part of the game's ground-target taxonomy.
- **Airborne early-warning aircraft (AEW)** — Aircraft carrying radar to detect threats beyond a ground station's horizon; AWACS is a more specific system with command/control functions.
- **Bomber** — Aircraft primarily configured to deliver ordnance against surface targets; usually less agile than a fighter.
- **Defended asset** — Airbase, tanker, convoy, ship or other friendly object that enemy forces may try to destroy.
- **Fighter / interceptor** — Aircraft designed to fight other aircraft; an *interceptor* emphasizes quickly reaching incoming hostile aircraft.
- **Flanker / Su-27** — NATO reporting name and model of a Soviet/Russian fighter; the original EF2000 manual identifies it as a projected major opponent.
- **Fulcrum / MiG-29** — NATO reporting name and model of a Soviet/Russian fighter; likewise a prominent EF2000 threat.
- **Helicopter / rotorcraft** — Aircraft supported by a powered rotor rather than fixed wings; often used for attack or transport.
- **MiG / Mikoyan** — Prefix associated with Soviet/Russian Mikoyan-designed aircraft, such as the MiG-29; not a general word meaning any Russian fighter.
- **NATO reporting name** — Western code name assigned to foreign aircraft and equipment, such as *Fulcrum* or *Flanker*, to ease identification.
- **SAM vehicle / mobile launcher** — Ground-based air-defense launcher that can relocate, complicating attack planning.
- **Ship / surface contact** — Maritime target on radar or JTIDS; EF2000's Sea Eagle mode is intended for ship engagements.
- **Strike aircraft** — Aircraft principally assigned surface-attack missions, sometimes able to defend itself in air-to-air combat.
- **Su / Sukhoi** — Prefix used for Sukhoi-designed Soviet/Russian aircraft, such as the Su-27.
- **Tanker / refueller** — Support aircraft carrying fuel for fighters; a high-value asset despite lacking a combat role.
- **Transport / support aircraft** — Aircraft assigned logistics, personnel movement, surveillance or other non-fighter roles.

## 11. DOS game, graphics, controls and simulator technology

- **AdLib / OPL3** — Family of DOS-era FM-synthesis music hardware and Yamaha sound chips supported by EF2000's audio configuration.
- **AI (Artificial Intelligence)** — In-game decision logic driving computer-controlled units; EF2000's branded components include WARGEN and SMARTPILOTS.
- **Block / version 2.0–2.05** — Names used for later DOS update levels; features, radar controls and key bindings differ from the original 1995 release.
- **CD-ROM** — Optical-disc medium used to distribute EF2000 and, in some configurations, its audio/content.
- **Capture / screenshot** — Saved image of the game display. Later DOS versions offer an `.LBM` screen capture command.
- **Conventional memory** — The first 640 KiB of the original IBM PC memory map used by DOS and programs; EF2000 still needed some of this memory free even though its 32-bit engine used more memory elsewhere.
- **DOS (Disk Operating System)** — The IBM-PC operating environment for the original game, distinct from Windows-native *Super EF2000*.
- **DOS/4GW** — DOS protected-mode runtime/driver used by many mid-1990s PC games to run 32-bit code while launching from DOS.
- **EMS (Expanded Memory Specification)** — DOS-era mechanism used to access memory beyond conventional memory; EF2000's original requirements include several megabytes of EMS.
- **Frame rate (FPS)** — Images rendered per second; affects perceived smoothness and responsiveness but not necessarily simulation time.
- **Gamma correction** — Adjustment of image brightness response without changing every pixel equally; some DOS versions have gamma hotkeys.
- **Gravis UltraSound (GUS)** — Popular 1990s PC sound card supported by EF2000, offering sample-based playback and digital audio features.
- **HOTAS (Hands On Throttle And Stick)** — Control philosophy and hardware giving pilots major functions without releasing the throttle or stick; 1990s Thrustmaster controllers provided EF2000 mappings.
- **HUD-only view** — EF2000 presentation showing the outside world and HUD without the visible cockpit frame.
- **Joystick axis / button** — Analog or digital control input; in EF2000 may command pitch/roll/throttle/rudder and weapon release.
- **Keypad / KP** — Separate numeric cluster on a PC keyboard; the shortcut reference uses `KP 1` etc. for cockpit and panel controls.
- **LBM / ILBM (InterLeaved BitMap)** — Bitmap picture format from the Amiga/Deluxe Paint ecosystem, used for EF2000 screenshots in later DOS builds. The underlying *IFF* file-container name is **not** military IFF (Identification Friend or Foe).
- **LAN (Local Area Network)** — Network connecting local computers for multiplayer; original EF2000 also offered serial/network play.
- **MIDI (Musical Instrument Digital Interface)** — Music event/protocol standard used by the game's synthesizer and sound-card setup; MIDI note data is not digitized speech.
- **Multiplayer / network game** — Multiple human-controlled pilots in the simulated environment, potentially with extra label and chat shortcuts.
- **Night vision (NV)** — Display aid for seeing in low-light conditions; EF2000's night-vision toggle differs from the passive IRST target sensor.
- **Padlock camera** — Game camera that keeps a selected object in view, including specialized aircraft/missile modes; see section 3.
- **Protected mode** — CPU operating mode enabling larger memory address spaces and protection mechanisms, used by 32-bit DOS games via extenders.
- **Sound Blaster / AWE32** — Creative Labs PC sound-card family supported by EF2000 for digitized effects and/or music synthesis.
- **SVGA (Super Video Graphics Array)** — PC graphics term covering resolutions beyond basic VGA; EF2000 can switch graphics modes and detail levels.
- **Simulation time / time acceleration** — The fictional world's clock, which EF2000 can speed up or advance to an event; separate from graphics rendering speed.
- **Texture mapping** — Applying a sampled bitmap to a polygonal surface. EF2000's terrain and aircraft graphics use this approach extensively.
- **Softkey mapping** — Context-sensitive association between keyboard combinations and an active MFD's on-screen button functions.
- **TACTCOM** — DOS expansion to EF2000; distinct from *Super EF2000* and later *Eurofighter Typhoon* products.
- **VESA / VBE (Video Electronics Standards Association / VESA BIOS Extensions)** — Standardized interface that DOS games used to access higher-resolution SVGA modes.
- **VGA (Video Graphics Array)** — IBM PC graphics standard; the shortcut reference distinguishes VGA and higher-resolution SVGA modes.
- **View browse / fly-by / external** — Game-only camera modes selecting different aircraft or showing the player's aircraft passing the camera.
- **Virtual cockpit** — Rendered 3D cockpit with movable view; does not imply VR headset equipment.
- **x87 / FPU (Floating-Point Unit)** — Hardware floating-point processor used for demanding 3D mathematics. EF2000's DOS requirements expected an x87-equipped 486/Pentium-class CPU.

## 12. Units and frequent abbreviations

| Marking | Meaning | Practical interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| `°` / deg | Degrees | Angular measure: heading, bearing, bank or radar sector. A full circle is 360°. |
| `ft` | Feet | Common altitude and runway-height unit; 1 ft = 0.3048 m. |
| `ft/min` / `fpm` | Feet per minute | Climb or descent rate. |
| `G` | Multiples of gravity | Load factor; 1 G is normal gravitational acceleration. |
| `km` | Kilometres | 1 km = 1,000 m. |
| `kt`, `kts`, `kn` | Knots | Speed: 1 knot = 1 nautical mile per hour ≈ 1.852 km/h. |
| `Mach` / `M` | Mach number | Ratio to local speed of sound, not a fixed km/h. |
| `m`, `km` | Metres, kilometres | Used for distance or some target-range displays. |
| `NM`, `nmi` | Nautical miles | Distance: 1 NM = 1.852 km ≈ 1.151 statute miles. |
| `rpm` | Revolutions per minute | Rotational rate, often used in engine instrumentation. |
| `s` / `sec` | Seconds | Time or countdowns. |

**Potentially confusing double meanings:** `AP` may mean autopilot; `AB` afterburner; `AA` can mean air-to-air (EF2000's weapon context) but **AAA** means anti-aircraft artillery; `AG` means air-to-ground; `DASS` is defensive avionics, while `ECM` is one part of electronic defense; `IFF` means friend-or-foe identification in aviation but is also the name of an image-file container family; `track` can mean flight path or radar contact; and a *padlock* is a camera view rather than a weapon lock. A `2.0` or `2.01` "block" in period EF2000 notes is a **software release/build**, not necessarily a real-world aircraft production block.
